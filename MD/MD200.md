## General parameters

MD200 is a high-current brushless motor controller targeted at robotic actuators with high motor
phase current and power requirements, such as the
[AKE90-8](https://www.mabrobotics.pl/product-page/ake90-8-cubemars-t-motor). It shares the control
algorithm (FOC), encoder, FDCAN communication interface and operation modes (position PID, velocity
PID, impedance, profile position and profile velocity) with the [MD80](#md80), so all software
tools and configuration procedures described for MD80 apply to MD200 as well.

## Power supply

```{warning}
Always make sure that the polarity of the power supply is correct. MD200 does not have reverse
polarity protection, so connecting the power supply in reverse polarity will cause permanent damage
to the controller.
```

```{warning}
A precharge circuit must be implemented between the power source (e.g. battery) and MD200 to limit
the inrush current into the controller's DC bus capacitors when the supply is connected.
```

```{warning}
When braking, MD200 feeds energy back to the DC bus (regenerative braking). On the test dynamometer
MD200 fed up to 858 W back to the supply. The power source must be able to absorb this energy (e.g.
a battery), or a braking resistor must be used. A power supply that cannot sink current will let the
bus voltage rise, and exceeding the maximum input voltage of 60 V will damage the controller.
```

```{figure} images/magnet_encoder_asm.png
Magnet - encoder placement cross section
```

The optimal height between the magnet and the encoder IC is 1mm. The magnet and the encoder must be
on the same rotation axis.

- The power supply and the motor phases are connected to M4 threaded terminals. Tighten the
  terminal screws with a torque of 1.4 Nm. MD200 has two positive (V+) and two ground (GND) supply
  terminals, connected in parallel, for easy daisy-chaining of the power supply between controllers.

### CAN FD

MD200 has two CAN FD connectors (Molex Micro-Lock Plus, PCB header 505567-0371, mating housing
[505565-0301](https://www.molex.com/en-us/products/part-detail/5055650301)). Both connectors are
connected in parallel for easy daisy-chaining. The CAN FD port is not isolated. The termination
resistor can be mounted on the solder pads marked TERM if necessary.

```{warning}
Applying supply voltage to these pins will cause permanent damage to the controller! </font>
```

### Thermistor connection

MD200 measures the MOSFET and motor temperature in the same way as MD80. The motor shutdown
temperature is configurable up to 140°C max with a hysteresis of 20°C. The driver shutdown
temperature is fixed at 100°C with a hysteresis of 20°C. The MOSFET thermistor is built in directly
under the power stage and only the motor thermistor pads (marked MOTOR TEMP) are available.

```{note}
We recommend using [MABs thermistors](https://www.mabrobotics.pl/product-page/thermistor). Using
other thermistors may result in imprecise temperature readout.
```

### Aux connectors

MD200 has the same auxiliary connectors as MD80.

**Aux 1 connector (SPI)** is dedicated to connect MABs
[ME_am](https://www.mabrobotics.pl/product-page/me-am) and other SPI based encoders (_530480650
Molex PicoBlade_).

**Aux 2 connector (RS422/GPIO)** has two user-configurable GPIOs and an RS422 interface for
connecting to the RLS Axim2 encoder (_530480810 Molex PicoBlade_). AUX GPIO A pin can be used as an
automatic brake control output signal, the same way as on MD80. See [Brakes](/MD/brakes) for
details.

```{warning}
<font color='red'>While auxiliary connector pins are 5V tolerant, we recommend using 3.3V logic if
possible. </font>
```

## Thermal characteristics

**Test conditions and setup**

MD200 V1.0 was tested at supply voltages of 24, 36, 48 and 60 V in two setups:

- inductive load - star-connected inductors simulating a motor winding,
- dynamometer - QS205 hub motor braked by MD200, driven by an AKE90-8 load motor.

Each run held a constant phase current for up to 120 s. A run ended either after 120 s or when the
driver over-temperature protection tripped at 100°C. All tests were performed in free air at approx.
20°C ambient temperature, with no heatsink and no forced airflow. The temperature was measured with
the onboard power stage thermistor.

```{note}
The continuous current values below are the highest currents at which MD200 completed 120 s without
thermal cutoff. In some of these runs the driver temperature was still rising at the end of the run.
With a heatsink or forced airflow, higher continuous currents can be achieved.
```

```{list-table}
---
header-rows: 1
---
* - Quantity
  - Inductive load
  - Dynamometer
* - Continuous phase current (120 s, no thermal cutoff)
  - 70 A at 24 - 48 V, 60 A at 60 V
  - 70 A at 24 - 48 V, 60 A at 60 V
* - Time to thermal cutoff at 100 A
  - 40 - 71 s
  - 44 s (48 V)
* - Time to thermal cutoff at 150 A
  - 15 - 19 s
  - 14 - 19 s
* - Time to thermal cutoff at 210 A
  - 8.8 - 10 s
  - -
* - Max. mechanical power
  - -
  - 1584 W (60 V, 150 A)
* - Max. power fed back to the supply
  - -
  - 858 W (60 V, 90 A)
```

The AKE90-8 drove the shaft while MD200 braked the QS205 at the set phase current, from 10 A to
150 A in 10 A steps. Mechanical power is calculated from the torque estimated by MD200 and the

(md200_peak_current)=

### Peak current

The maximum phase current available to users is limited to 200 A.

The time for which MD200 can deliver a high phase current is limited by the driver over-temperature
protection (100°C). Starting from approx. 30°C in free air, the inductive load test gave the
following times to thermal cutoff:

```{list-table}
---
header-rows: 1
---
* - Phase current
  - Time to thermal cutoff
* - 100 A
  - 40 - 71 s (60 V - 24 V)
* - 150 A
  - 15 - 19 s (60 V - 24 V)
* - 200 A
  - approx. 10 s (all supply voltages)
```

Above approx. 170 A the time to cutoff hardly depends on the supply voltage. A higher starting
temperature shortens these times, and a heatsink or forced airflow lengthens them.
